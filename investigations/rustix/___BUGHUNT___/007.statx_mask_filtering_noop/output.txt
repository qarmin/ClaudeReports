(Reproduced identically across multiple runs - StatxFlags::all().bits() is a
compile-time-constant expression, not runtime-dependent, so this is fully
deterministic. The live statx() call itself still succeeds on this kernel
because the kernel silently ignores the unknown bit today - the guard exists
to protect against a FUTURE kernel that assigns it a real meaning, exactly
as the sourcing PR #1656 states: "This restores the future-kernel safety
boundary intended by #379. It does not claim a vulnerability on current
kernels.")

No `strace` was available in this container (`which strace` found nothing)
to directly capture the raw syscall argument bytes; the "unfiltered bit
passing through" claim is instead demonstrated via the identical
source-level expression (`mask & StatxFlags::all()`) used verbatim at both
backend call sites (src/backend/linux_raw/fs/syscalls.rs:840 and
src/backend/libc/fs/syscalls.rs:2091), which is the more direct and
reliable evidence here.
